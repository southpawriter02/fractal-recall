# Skaldic Year-Naming: Oral Tradition and Collective Memory

**Document ID:** CUL-SKALDIC-YEAR-v1.0

## 1) Introduction and scope

Skaldic Year-Naming transforms abstract chronology into **cultural memory infrastructure**, converting Dvergr numerical precision ("Year 783 PG") into commemorative narratives preserving collective experience ("The Year of Counted Breaths"). While Dvergr notation provides legal coordination precision, year-names enable **oral tradition transmission**—populations without literacy can recite chronological sequences through poetic commemorations, preserving historical continuity across generations despite limited written record access.

The system operates as **parallel calendar overlay** rather than replacement: Dvergr Year.Month.Day notation remains legally binding for Guild arbitration and treaty enforcement, while Skaldic year-names provide cultural annotation. A contract signed "Year 783 PG (The Year of Counted Breaths)" employs Dvergr number as operative legal reference with year-name appended as mnemonic aid—should arbitration disputes arise years later, Dvergr number ensures precise dating regardless of whether parties remember year-name significance.

### Why commemorative naming matters

**Memory transmission without literacy:** Combine populations exhibit ~40-60% literacy rates (ability to read/write Dvergr numerals and basic contract language), but oral tradition competence approaches universal. A Combine farmer unable to read "783.7.15" on written contract can nonetheless recite "The Year of Counted Breaths, Harvest-Prime, Day 15" through verbal instruction—year-name provides mnemonic anchor enabling illiterate populations to reference chronology accurately.

**Historical preservation through oral recitation:** "My grandmother born The Year Walls Became Creeds, my father born The Year of Three Jarls Fell, I born The Year of Ash-Snow"—three-generation family history compressed into memorable sequence without requiring numerical literacy.

**Cultural significance encoding:** Dvergr numerical years carry no inherent meaning—"Year 783 PG" communicates temporal position but provides no context regarding historical significance. Year-name "The Year of Counted Breaths" immediately signals shared cultural understanding: populations across Midgard Combine recognize reference to respiratory plague outbreak (Year 783 PG, Months 2-5), mortality crisis requiring breath-counting ritual verification (distinguishing living from dying), communal trauma defining year's cultural identity.

**Collective memory consolidation:** Skaldic Moot year-naming process (annual gathering, Month 13 Days 25-29) serves **social consensus function**—communities deliberate which events merit commemorative preservation, establishing shared historical narrative through majority vote ratification.

### Operational vs cultural coordination distinction

Year-naming operates in **oral tradition domain**, complementing rather than competing with Dvergr numerical system's **legal coordination domain**. Dvergr notation enables precise treaty timestamps, contract enforcement, and arbitration—functions requiring unambiguous temporal reference. Year-names enable historical recitation, generational continuity, and cultural identity maintenance—functions requiring emotional resonance and memorability. The dual-system architecture reflects functional specialization: numbers for precision, names for memory.

## 2) Year-naming process and Skaldic Moot

### Annual Moot convening — timing and logistics

**Timing:** Skaldic Moot convenes **Month 13, Days 25-29** (final five days of Dvergr calendar year, immediately preceding anomalous day or New Year transition). Timing serves dual function: (1) year's events completed—communities possess complete annual knowledge enabling informed year-name selection, and (2) proximity to Year's-End Feast (Month 13 Day 30 on anomalous years) enables Moot to double as festival gathering, maximizing attendance through cultural/social incentives beyond pure naming obligation.

**Location rotation:** Moot location rotates annually among designated host settlements—typically Combine Hold seats, Rangers Guild waystations with festival infrastructure, or historically significant sites (battlefields, Age transition commemorations, legendary hero birthplaces). Rotation prevents single-settlement dominance, distributes economic benefits (hosting Moot generates lodging revenue, merchant traffic, regional prestige), and enables diverse populations to participate without requiring repeated long-distance travel to centralized permanent location.

**Attendance composition:**
- **Chronicler's Council delegates:** 12-18 official representatives (elders, historians, oral tradition masters) holding institutional authority to ratify year-name
- **Skaldic band representatives:** 40-80 delegates from nomadic/semi-nomadic Skaldic communities preserving pre-Glitch oral tradition lineage
- **Combine Hold delegates:** 60-120 representatives from agricultural settlements (typically Hold councils send 2-4 delegates each)
- **Rangers Guild observers:** 8-12 Guild coordinators (provide logistical support, document proceedings, ensure concordance with Dvergr calendar)
- **Open attendance:** 200-500 additional participants (merchants, artisans, farmers, travelers) attending for cultural/festival purposes without formal voting rights

Total attendance: 300-700 individuals depending on host location accessibility, weather conditions, concurrent festivals (anomalous day Years-End Feast typically doubles attendance).

### Proposal submission process

**Open submission period:** Any Moot attendee may propose year-names—no requirement for formal authority, Chronicler's Council membership, or cultural credentials. This egalitarian access reflects oral tradition philosophy: historical memory belongs to communities collectively, not elite institutions exclusively.

**Submission requirements:**
1. **Proposed name:** Complete year-name phrase (typically 3-8 words, rarely exceeding 10)
2. **Historical justification:** Explanation of commemorated events, their significance, and why they merit annual designation
3. **Poetic rationale:** Defense of name's memorability, emotional resonance, metaphorical effectiveness
4. **Regional support:** Evidence that proposed name reflects broader community experience rather than isolated local concern (minimum 3-5 supporting delegates from different settlements/regions)

### Deliberation criteria and debate structure

**Deliberation Days 27-28:** Chronicler's Council facilitates structured debate examining each proposal against four evaluation criteria:

**Criterion 1 — Historical significance:** Does commemorated event merit annual designation? Threshold: event must affect ≥20% of Combine/Skaldic populations, demonstrate lasting impact beyond immediate year, or represent pivotal historical transition.

**Criterion 2 — Memorability and poetic quality:** Does name employ mnemonic devices (alliteration, metaphor, emotional resonance) enhancing oral transmission? Strong proposals use concrete imagery over abstract concepts, rhythmic phrasing over awkward syllable patterns, emotional weight over neutral descriptors.

**Criterion 3 — Cultural consensus:** Does name reflect shared understanding across diverse populations rather than narrow factional perspective? Proposals privileging single-settlement experiences over pan-Combine relevance face rejection.

**Criterion 4 — Historical precedent compatibility:** Does name maintain continuity with established naming patterns? Proposals radically diverging from traditional poetic structures face skepticism despite meeting other criteria.

### Voting protocols and ratification

**Day 29 ratification ceremony:** Final voting determines authoritative year-name for upcoming year (Moot occurring Month 13 Year 782 PG selects name for Year 783 PG, proclaimed New Year Day 1).

**Voting eligibility:** Only Chronicler's Council delegates and formal regional representatives (Skaldic band, Combine Hold, Rangers Guild) cast ratification votes—total voting body 120-200 individuals.

**Voting procedure:**
1. **Finalist presentation:** Each remaining proposal receives final 10-minute advocacy (proposer makes closing argument, Council delegate summarizes strengths)
2. **Deliberation pause:** 30-minute recess enabling voters to consult, reconsider positions, negotiate consensus
3. **First ballot:** Simple plurality vote among finalists. If one proposal receives ≥60% support, ratification complete. If no proposal reaches 60% threshold, runoff between top two candidates
4. **Runoff ballot (if necessary):** Binary choice between strongest proposals. Majority (>50%) determines winner
5. **Unanimous affirmation:** Regardless of vote margin, all delegates vocally affirm ratified name (ceremonial unity display, prevents ongoing factional disputes undermining year-name authority)

**Ratification proclamation:** Immediately following vote, Council Chair proclaims year-name to assembled Moot—"By consensus of the gathered communities, the coming year shall be known as [Year-Name]!" Proclamation triggers celebration (feasting, music, storytelling), Year's-End Feast commencement (on anomalous day years), and formal documentation (Council scribes record ratified name, associated Dvergr year, historical justification for archive preservation).

## 4) Chronicler's Council and concordance maintenance

### Council composition and authority structure

**Membership:** Chronicler's Council comprises **18 permanent members** serving lifetime appointments (or until voluntary retirement, typically age 70-80+):
- **6 Skaldic Elders:** Master oral tradition practitioners from nomadic Skaldic bands, preserving pre-Glitch commemorative calendaring lineage
- **6 Combine Chroniclers:** Historians and scribes from agricultural settlements, typically Hold council-appointed positions representing regional populations
- **4 Rangers Guild Coordinators:** Guild personnel managing logistical support, Dvergr concordance verification, and distribution networks
- **2 Dvergr Representatives:** Timekeeper's Guild delegates ensuring year-name system maintains compatibility with Dvergr numerical calendar for legal coordination

**Selection process:** Vacancies filled through nomination and confirmation (relevant constituency nominates candidate based on expertise; current Council members interview assessing qualifications; 2/3 majority vote confirms appointment).

### Year-name registry and concordance maintenance

**Authoritative registry:** Council maintains comprehensive year-name documentation spanning 601+ years (Year 182 PG forward):
- **Ratified names:** Official year-designation for each Dvergr calendar year
- **Historical justification:** Events commemorated, their significance, why Moot selected name
- **Voting records:** Delegate counts, approval percentages, finalist alternatives considered
- **Regional variants:** Non-official names used by specific populations, geographic distribution
- **Poetic analysis:** Mnemonic devices employed (alliteration, metaphor, rhythm), memorability assessment

**Archive preservation:** Primary registry maintained in vellum-bound codices (climate-controlled Guild vault storage, Combine Hold seat location rotating with Council Chair annually). **Duplicate registries** distributed to 8 regional Combine Holds and 3 Dvergr stronghold libraries—redundant preservation ensures survival despite catastrophic events (fire, flood, military raids).

**Annual concordance publication:** Post-Moot (within 30-60 days), Council publishes year-name concordance distributed through Rangers Guild Messenger networks enabling populations unable to attend Moot to adopt ratified names.

## 5) Adoption patterns and dual-notation practices

### Population familiarity rates across demographics

**Combine core territories (≥80% familiarity):** Midgard Combine populations exhibit highest year-name recognition—≥80% of adults can recite current year-name and 3-5 most recent predecessors, ≥60% can recite 10+ year sequence. Familiarity driven by: (1) oral tradition cultural continuity (Skaldic heritage populations maintaining commemorative practices), (2) annual Moot proximity (most Combine settlements within 200 km of rotating Moot locations, enabling periodic attendance), (3) Hold council promotion (local authorities encourage year-name adoption strengthening cultural identity), and (4) Rangers Messenger distribution (concordance notices reaching 85%+ of literate populations within 90 days).

**Demographic breakdown (Combine territories):**
- **Elders (60+ years):** 95%+ current year-name familiarity, 80%+ can recite 20+ year sequence through lived experience
- **Adults (25-60 years):** 85%+ current year-name familiarity, 65%+ recite 10+ year sequence
- **Youth (10-25 years):** 70%+ current year-name familiarity, 40%+ recite 5+ year sequence (lower rates reflect limited historical perspective, education still developing)
- **Children (<10 years):** 40%+ current year-name familiarity, primarily through parental instruction

**Literacy correlation:** Year-name familiarity shows **inverse correlation** with literacy rates—illiterate populations exhibit 90%+ year-name recognition (rely on oral tradition for chronological reference) while highly literate populations (scribes, merchants, Guild officials) show 70%+ recognition (comfortable using Dvergr numerical notation, less dependent on mnemonic year-names).

### Oral versus written context usage patterns

**Oral contexts (≥75% year-name usage):** Spoken temporal references overwhelmingly employ year-names over Dvergr numbers: "My daughter born The Year of Ash-Snow" vs "My daughter born Year 702 PG" (ratio ~90:10 year-names to numbers).

**Written contexts (≥85% Dvergr notation primacy):** Written documents prioritize Dvergr numerical precision with optional year-name annotation: legal contracts (mandatory Dvergr date, optional year-name appended parenthetically), Guild manifests (Dvergr timestamp for operational coordination), historical records (dual-notation throughout), personal correspondence (literate individuals typically use Dvergr dates).

**Code-switching patterns:** Populations demonstrate **context-sensitive calendar switching**—same individual uses year-names in oral conversation, Dvergr numbers in written contracts, seamlessly transitioning without conscious deliberation. This bilingual chronological competence enables cultural participation (year-name oral tradition) while maintaining legal coordination (Dvergr numerical precision).

### Dual-notation contract conventions

**Standard contract format:** Guild arbitration courts recognize three acceptable date notations:

**Format 1 — Dvergr primary, year-name parenthetical:**
> "Contract executed Year 783 PG (The Year of Counted Breaths), Month 7, Day 15"

Most common format (≥70% of Guild-arbitrated contracts). Dvergr number provides legally operative date, year-name adds cultural context.

**Format 2 — Pure Dvergr notation:**
> "Contract executed Year 783 PG, Month 7, Day 15"

Acceptable format (≥25% of contracts), typically used by Dvergr merchants, Rangers Guild operations, or parties prioritizing brevity.

**Format 3 — Year-name with Dvergr concordance reference:**
> "Contract executed The Year of Counted Breaths (Year 783 PG per Chronicler's Council concordance), Harvest-Prime, Day 15"

Rare format (≥5% of contracts), used when parties prioritize oral tradition aesthetics but require legal validity.
