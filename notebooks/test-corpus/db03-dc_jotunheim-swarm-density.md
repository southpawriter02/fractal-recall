## Survey Summary

This comprehensive population survey documents the active growth of Jötunheim's Insectoid-scale drone populations across primary habitat zones during a seven-day observation window in early summer, Year 783 PG. The survey provides critical evidence that the Swarm represents an expanding threat rather than a static post-Glitch remnant, with three confirmed replication sites producing substantial numbers of new drones daily.

---

## Survey Design and Methodology

The Rangers Guild and Scriptorium-Primus Joint Cartography Division deployed a multi-point observation grid across Jötunheim's primary Swarm habitat zones. Cartographer Skald Bergson led the operation, coordinating eight Ranger scouts and four Scribes organized into three rotating teams. The survey covered the Knuckle-Bone Fields, the Great Looms Corridor, and the Iron Heart Breach—representing the overwhelming majority of known Swarm concentration areas.

Observation points were established at elevated positions atop collapsed structures and gantry platforms, maintaining substantial stand-off distances from Swarm activity zones. Each team rotated through multiple observation points daily, conducting systematic watches at dawn, midday, and dusk to capture diurnal activity variations. The multi-day survey duration and multiple observation points provided extensive coverage across the target zones.

Measurement protocols combined visual density estimation with acoustic signature monitoring. Observers assessed drone concentrations within sample areas, noting movement patterns and flow directions using traditional navigation tools. Ambient chittering volume served as a secondary density indicator, with quiet baseline sounds suggesting sparse activity and overwhelming industrial cacophony marking extreme concentration zones.

## Population Distribution Patterns

The survey revealed dramatic variations in Swarm density across Jötunheim's salvage zones, with certain sectors showing concentration levels that suggest permanent infrastructure rather than transient scavenging behavior.

### Knuckle-Bone Fields Primary Habitat

The northwestern sectors displayed high salvage activity, with dense drone formations actively processing collapsed infrastructure. Northern patrol zones showed moderate densities, while the northeastern sector exhibited extreme concentration levels strongly suggesting a replication facility. Eastern and southeastern zones varied from moderate salvage operations to sparse coverage in areas with limited remaining salvageable materials.

Southern patrol corridors maintained moderate drone presence, while the southwestern sector showed evidence of recent population surges. Western zones near forge complexes demonstrated consistently high activity levels. Across the Knuckle-Bone Fields as a whole, the survey teams estimated drone populations in the range of well over a million individuals, with density patterns suggesting organized territorial control rather than random scavenging behavior.

### Great Looms Corridor Secondary Habitat

The Great Looms Corridor demonstrated even more intense concentration patterns. The northern sector showed extreme density levels with clear indicators of active replication facilities. Central transport corridors displayed high activity combining salvage operations with organized material movement patterns. Southern zones maintained high salvage intensity.

Overall, the Looms Corridor represents the highest concentration zone identified during the survey, with drone populations approaching or exceeding a million individuals compressed into a more compact geographic area. The density levels and behavioral patterns observed here strongly suggest this zone serves as the primary manufacturing hub for the entire Swarm network.

### Iron Heart Breach Tertiary Habitat

The Iron Heart Breach demonstrated the most extreme concentration levels documented anywhere in Jötunheim. The breach core, centered on an active Aetheric reactor complex, showed overwhelming drone density that rendered close observation impossible. This site represents a confirmed and highly active replication facility with apparent access to unlimited power supplies.

### Aggregate Population Assessment

Across all survey zones, the teams documented Swarm populations that clearly number in the millions of individual drones. Extrapolating from the survey coverage to include unsurveyed portions of Jötunheim suggests total planetary populations potentially reaching several million drones. These figures represent active, growing populations rather than declining remnants of pre-Glitch systems.

## Replication Site Analysis

The survey identified three sites with behavioral and environmental signatures strongly indicating active drone fabrication capabilities.

### Site 1: Knuckle-Bone Fields Northeast Sector

A collapsed forge complex in the northeastern Knuckle-Bone Fields exhibits unusual Swarm behavior patterns. The site shows abnormally high drone concentrations despite limited surface salvage opportunities. Most significantly, observers documented continuous drone traffic entering sub-level access points, with many individuals carrying salvaged materials *into* the complex rather than away from it—a complete reversal of normal Swarm foraging patterns.

The site generates overwhelming acoustic signatures resembling industrial manufacturing rather than ordinary salvage activity. Observers noted drones emerging from the sub-level facilities with pristine carapaces showing no oxidation or wear patterns, suggesting recent fabrication rather than activation of ancient dormant units.

The forge infrastructure remains partially intact, and Aetheric power cell signatures suggest the complex retains functional energy supplies. If the site operates as a replication facility, production rates likely reach hundreds or potentially over a thousand new drones daily.

### Site 2: Great Looms Corridor Northern Sector

The Great Loom Fabricator represents the most unambiguous evidence of active large-scale drone production. This intact Jötun-Forged-scale manufacturing facility displays all the characteristics of fully operational industrial capacity. The site maintains the highest drone densities documented anywhere in the survey, with massive stockpiles of organized salvage materials surrounding the complex.

Observers witnessed what appeared to be assembly line operations, with drones moving in precise parallel formations suggesting manufacturing workflow rather than chaotic scavenging. The Great Loom structure itself radiates visible Aetheric energy, confirming operational power systems. Most dramatically, teams observed continuous emergence of new drones from the fabrication complex at rates suggesting substantial daily production—potentially tens of thousands of new units.

The site operates as a self-sustaining cycle: salvage materials flow in from across Jötunheim, the Loom processes these materials into new drones, and fresh units deploy outward to gather more salvage. This represents the nightmare scenario long theorized but never previously confirmed—a fully autonomous replication system requiring no external control or guidance.

### Site 3: Iron Heart Breach Core

The Iron Heart Aetheric Reactor site represents the most troubling discovery of the entire survey. The reactor complex not only remains operational after over eight centuries but actively supports the largest confirmed drone population concentration in Jötunheim. The blue-white Aetheric glow extends across substantial surrounding territory, providing both visual confirmation of reactor status and likely explaining the extreme energy availability supporting replication operations.

Observers documented what appeared to be developmental stages, with very small drones clustered near the reactor gradually growing to medium and then full scale over periods spanning several days. This suggests not just assembly-line fabrication but potentially some form of growth or maturation process enabled by the intense Aetheric field.

The reactor's pre-Glitch operational ratings suggest centuries of remaining functional lifespan at current output levels. This single site potentially produces more new drones daily than all other confirmed replication facilities combined—possibly reaching many tens of thousands of new units every day under optimal conditions.

## Swarm Movement Patterns

Survey teams documented organized traffic flows between major zones, suggesting coordinated behavior rather than independent action by individual drones. Primary corridors connect the Knuckle-Bone Fields with the Great Looms complex, with heavily laden drones moving salvage materials toward manufacturing sites while fresh drones deploy outward toward new salvage opportunities.

A second major corridor links the Iron Heart reactor zone with the Great Looms, possibly supplying newly fabricated drones or specialized manufacturing materials. These traffic patterns maintain consistent flow throughout daylight hours, with movements following established routes rather than dispersing randomly across available terrain.

Movement velocities vary by load state, with unburdened drones traveling noticeably faster than individuals carrying salvage materials. Activity patterns show diurnal variation, with intense morning surges followed by peak midday transport activity. Evening activity levels remain substantial though somewhat reduced. Nocturnal observations suggest reduced but ongoing activity throughout darkness, though whether this represents actual rest cycles or simply reduced observer visibility remains uncertain.

## Population Growth Dynamics

The three confirmed replication sites demonstrate combined production capacity that profoundly alters threat assessment calculations. Site 1 in the Knuckle-Bone Fields likely produces hundreds to over a thousand new drones daily. Site 2 at the Great Looms potentially produces tens of thousands daily. Site 3 at the Iron Heart Breach may produce many tens of thousands daily under optimal conditions.

Combined maximum production across all three sites could theoretically exceed many tens of thousands of new drones per day. Even accounting for substantial attrition from environmental damage, operational malfunctions, and occasional human interdiction efforts, net population growth likely remains strongly positive.

Daily population increases potentially number in the tens of thousands. Monthly growth could add over a million new drones. Annual theoretical maximum growth—limited primarily by available salvage materials rather than manufacturing capacity—might reach many millions of new units.

The critical implication: the Swarm population is not static. Jötunheim does not contain a fixed number of ancient drones slowly wearing out over centuries. Instead, the realm hosts an actively growing industrial ecosystem converting pre-Glitch ruins into exponentially increasing drone populations.

## Strategic Threat Assessment

Current threat levels remain contained to Jötunheim, but projection models suggest escalating danger timelines. Within several years, complete consumption of remaining Jötunheim salvage resources may force Swarm expansion toward adjacent realms. Within a decade, Midgard industrial zones and settlement peripheries could face direct Swarm pressure. Within a generation, multi-realm infestation scenarios become plausible, potentially triggering economic collapse as salvage operations become impossible across broad regions.

On timescales spanning several decades, the Swarm could theoretically pose planetary-scale threats if left completely unchecked, with accessible technology across all realms facing systematic consumption.

The Rangers Guild and Scriptorium jointly evaluated four mitigation approaches:

**Replication Site Destruction** was rapidly rejected as infeasible. The sites maintain overwhelming defensive drone concentrations, and any assault force would face catastrophic casualties. Even if successful, such operations would cost hundreds of lives while destroying only one or two facilities—and the Swarm likely operates additional undiscovered replication sites.

**Material Scarcity Strategy**, proposed by Dvergr trade partners, advocates aggressive pre-emptive salvage of high-value targets before the Swarm reaches them. This approach accepts that Jötunheim cannot be cleared but seeks to extract maximum value before complete realm consumption. Implementation would require substantial investment in high-risk salvage operations.

**Containment Doctrine**, favored by Rangers Guild leadership, advocates designating Jötunheim as forbidden territory and accepting permanent realm loss. This approach prioritizes preventing Swarm expansion rather than attempting to reclaim lost ground. Midgard defensive perimeters would monitor for any Swarm breakthrough attempts.

**Long-term Attrition** represents the Scriptorium perspective: Aetheric power cells eventually fail, even those rated for centuries of operation. Eventually—though potentially not for many generations—energy depletion will halt replication operations. This approach accepts current Swarm dominance while hoping civilization survives long enough to outlast Swarm operational capacity.

The joint recommendation combines Material Scarcity and Containment approaches: immediate high-risk salvage operations to extract remaining high-value materials, followed by long-term Jötunheim isolation with defensive monitoring along Midgard borders.
