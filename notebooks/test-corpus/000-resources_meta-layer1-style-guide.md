## Preface

This META entry consolidates the Layer 1 Resource Style Guide into the v4.0 World Bible. It governs voice, narrative style, and formatting for all Layer 1 materials. The core mandate is the Unbroken Facade: write as the world believes itself to be true, without winks to Layers 2 or 3.

---

## 1.0 Core Philosophy: The Unbroken Facade

- Layer 1 is the world's own self‑understanding. The mythology is history, science, law, and religion braided into one unquestioned narrative.
- There is no subtext, irony, or authorial aside. Gods are real. Giants were real. Magic is real.
- The reader's horror resides outside the text: knowing this is a tragic misreading of a dead technology.

## 2.0 Voice and Narrative Style (The Collective Voice)

Layer 1 texts are the work of culture, not individuals. Favor a collective, time‑polished voice over named authorship. Use one of the three sanctioned registers:

### 2.1 The Skaldic Voice (Sagas & Lays)

- **Tone:** Epic, formal, heroic, grim. Concerned with fate (Örlög) and great deeds.
- **Language:** Alliterative verse, kennings, vivid elemental imagery, percussive verbs.
- **Kennings examples:** blood = battle‑sweat, wound‑dew; sea = whale‑road, gannet's‑bath; warrior = feeder‑of‑ravens, shield‑oak; Blight = Serpent's‑Venom; ruins = Ymir's‑Bones.
- **Worldview:** Heroic fatalism. Virtue is to meet doom with honor.
- **Best for:** Creation myths, god‑sagas, epochal wars, Ragnarok lays.

### 2.2 The Priestly Voice (Creeds, Law‑Codes, Proverbs)

- **Tone:** Dogmatic, declarative, absolutely certain.
- **Language:** Simple, mnemonic, call‑and‑response; commandments, proverbs, triads.
- **Worldview:** Divinely ordered hierarchy. Gods above, mortals between, blighted below. Social stability is sacred.
- **Best for:** Creeds, law‑lists, moral codes, cosmological dicta.

#### Factional applications (Layer 1)

- **Iron‑Banes "Anvil‑Priest" voice:** aggressive, righteous, clinically brutal. Frames destruction as surgical purification.
- **God‑Sleepers "Pilgrim‑Prophet" voice:** devotional, awe‑filled, sorrowful patience. Populist hymns of the Grand Pilgrimage.

### 2.3 The Folk Voice (Songs, Fireside Tales)

- **Tone:** Familiar, practical, superstitious, intimate.
- **Language:** Simple, repetitive, rhymed or chanted; nature‑grounded metaphors.
- **Worldview:** An animistic world of local spirits and rules for survival.
- **Best for:** Work songs, cautionary rhymes, local legends, instructional tales.

### 2.4 Drill Prompts (Call‑and‑Response, Layer 1)

- **Definition:** A "Drill Prompt" is a fixed, chantable couplet or triad used to encode survival protocols or social rites for noisy environments.
- **Form:** One to three short lines. Imperatives. High redundancy. Optimized for bar‑noise or street‑crowd recall.
- **Example prompts:** "Eyes down. Hands home." "Pour the dead. Keep your head."

## 3.0 Formatting and Structure (Oral First)

- Assume oral tradition; written artifacts should reflect oral ancestry.
- Physical forms: Rune‑Stones, Carved Wooden Panels, Treated Hide Scrolls
- Organizational rules: No modern headers or academic subheads in‑diegesis
- Use repetition and fixed epithets to aid recall
- Prefer alliterative stress over rhyme; keep meter chantable

## 4.0 Resource Types and Use Cases

- **Saga:** Long, epic recounting of world‑shaping events. Poetic, formal.
- **Lay:** Focused poetic episode or explanatory myth. Sung or recited.
- **Creed / Law‑Code:** Declarative rules that encode moral and cosmic order.
- **Skaldic Song / Chant:** Performed pieces with clear rhythm and refrain.
- **Fireside Tale / Folk Tale:** Short prose teaching a local lesson or warning.

## 5.0 Implementation Notes (Production Guidance)

- Maintain the Unbroken Facade. Do not leak Layer 2 metaphors or Layer 3 terms.
- If a Layer 1 text must allude to forbidden knowledge, encode it mythically (e.g., "God‑Echo" for data‑ghost; "Bifröst Shards" for anomalous auroras).
- Localize vocabulary by region while preserving core doctrine.
- Keep alignment with faction portrayals.

## 7.0 Skaldic Voice Specification

**Voice Characteristics:**
- **Perspective:** Collective, time-polished narration
- **Tone:** Epic, formal, heroic, grim
- **Worldview:** Heroic fatalism

**Diction Requirements:**
- **Strong verbs:** Percussive, active, elemental
- **Elemental imagery:** Fire, frost, wind, stone, blood, sea
- **Fixed epithets:** Establish on first use, repeat for mnemonic aid
- **Prohibitions:** No L2 diagnostics, no L3 technical constructs, no author asides, no irony

**Cadence and Meter:**
- **Oral-first:** Read aloud to test; meter must hold at conversational breath
- **Line length:** 6–12 syllables typical
- **Alliteration:** Preferred over rhyme
- **Refrain structure:** 1–3 line refrains between verses

### 7.2 Approved Epithets and Kennings

**Person kennings:** feeder-of-ravens, shield-oak, road-wise one, war-wolf
**Blood kennings:** battle-sweat, wound-dew
**Sea kennings:** whale-road, gannet's-bath
**Ruins kennings:** Ymir's-Bones
**Blight kennings:** Serpent's-Venom
**Crafting kennings:** forge-song, bell-tongue, rope-law

### 7.3 Metaphor Bounds

**PERMIT these metaphor classes:**
- Elemental: Fire, frost, wind, stone, blood, bone, ash
- Craftwork: Forge, bell, rope, board, seam, anvil, hammer
- Beast & Road: Whale-road, wolf-wind, cart-path, raven-feast
- Oath & Doom: Cord, ring, bell, Örlög

**BAN these metaphor classes:**
- Technicisms: Machine, circuit, algorithm, protocol, spectrum, metric, sensor, data
- Bureaucratic diction: Compliance, optimization, policy, audit, stakeholder, deliverable
- Meta-language: Reader, author, narrative, text, document, chapter

### 7.4 Quote Styling Rules (Layer 1)

**Dialogue:** Short, declarative sentences; epithets on first entrance only

**Chant and Chorus:** Triads and refrains for memorability; keep breath countable

**Crowd Noise:** One-line calls or shouts

### 7.5 Layer Leakage Prevention

❌ **L3 leakage:** "Abort protocol engaged; variance ≥ 0.35."
✅ **L1 correction:** "The gods turned their faces. Örlög broke the thread."

### 7.6 Voice-Pure Snippet Examples

- **Battle:** "Raise the board and break the black wind."
- **Travel:** "Mark the whale-road in the mind; step the gannet's-bath in twos."
- **Trade:** "Spit the oath and weigh the ring."
- **Vigil:** "Bell once for watchers, twice for wolves."
- **Lament:** "When Ymir's-Bones groan, count the names, then close the door."

### 7.7 Pre-Publication Checklist

☐ **Unbroken Facade maintained:** No L2 diagnostics or L3 technical terms in body text
☐ **Kennings from approved classes:** All metaphors match permitted categories
☐ **First-mention links only:** v4.0 canon pages linked once per major section
☐ **Read-aloud test passed:** Cadence holds at conversational breath
☐ **Alfrune Lexicon alignment:** Hyphenation, capitalization, and fixed compounds verified

## 6.0 Practice Notes Aligned to Scriptorium Procedures (L1 Safety Speech)

### 6.1 Warning Scrolls (Common Notes in L1 Voice)

- **Purpose:** Public advisories written for villagers. Plain speech. No doctrine or predictions.
- **Structure:** When and Where, What We Saw, Do Not, Do, We Will Do
- **Style:** Skaldic or Folk simplicity. No numbers beyond small counts. No "analysis" language.

### 6.2 Ritual Shapes for Safety (Echoes of Geometry)

- Rope‑Baffle: Describe as a "rope circle" or "rope gate."
- Bell Cadence: Name the bells and the silence between.
- Quiet Hour: Call the attenuation window the "quiet hour" or "safe watch."

### 6.3 Prohibitions (Layer Discipline)

- Ban: "rune‑work," "protocol," "spectra," "ledger," "countersign," and "Codex" inside L1 texts.
- Keep the Unbroken Facade: if a concept requires explanation, replace with an accepted mythic kenning.
