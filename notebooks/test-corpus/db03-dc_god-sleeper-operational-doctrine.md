# God-Sleeper Operational Doctrine — Forge-Beast Encounters

## Minimal Log

**Date Window:** 783 PG, Spring
**Observer:** Jötun-Reader cultural liaison embedded with God-Sleeper pilgrimage

## Doctrinal Context

The God-Sleeper Creed maintains a distinctive theological position regarding the Svin-fylking—creatures they designate as "Forge-Beasts" and consider blessed rather than cursed. This stands in direct opposition to the Iron-Bane Crusade's characterization of the same creatures as victims of the Iron-Curse requiring destruction.

The scriptural foundation derives from *The Hymn of the Dreaming Forge*, which frames Svin-fylking as divine creations rather than Blight-corrupted fauna.

---

## Core Theological Position

### Divine Origin Theory

God-Sleeper teaching holds that Svin-fylking are "children of the Slumbering Forge-Gods." Adherents cite Iron Husk formation as evidence of divine blessing and interpret the creatures' coordinated intelligence as "god-touch"—evidence of supernatural guidance rather than mere animal instinct.

The doctrinal explanation holds that the Forge-Gods shaped these beasts to guard their sacred places during the Long Sleep. Consequently, Svin-fylking possess moral status as sacred beings, not to be hunted except in genuine self-defense.

### Sacred Geography

Observational data supports one element of God-Sleeper belief: Svin-fylking warbands frequently establish dens near Jötun-Forged sites. Whether this reflects the creatures' actual behavior patterns or selective interpretation by believers remains unclear.

God-Sleeper pilgrims interpret this proximity as evidence that the beasts "keep vigil over the Sleeping Gods." Encounters with warbands during pilgrimage are understood as tests of devotion. Notably, God-Sleeper shrines are often deliberately constructed near known den-sites—a practice that increases encounter frequency and reinforces doctrinal narratives.

---

## Operational Protocols

### Encounter Doctrine

God-Sleeper training emphasizes avoidance of provocation above all else. Pilgrims are taught to use low-frequency humming to signal non-threat—a technique that may have some basis in Svin-fylking acoustic perception, though verification remains incomplete.

Standard protocol calls for leaving iron scrap offerings at territory boundaries before entry. When encountering warbands directly, pilgrims are instructed to retreat slowly with lowered arms and avoid eye contact.

### Defensive Engagement

Defensive action is permitted only when directly charged or cornered, and doctrine mandates minimal force necessary to break engagement. Pursuit and killing are prohibited if any alternative exists.

Following any defensive engagement, pilgrims are expected to perform prayers of apology and leave offerings at the nearest shrine—a practice that reinforces the theological framework even when reality forces violent response.

### Sacred Harvest

The Creed permits collection of naturally shed tusks and abandoned Iron Husk fragments, but prohibits hunting, killing for salvage, or active harvest from living specimens. Found materials require consecration before use and are primarily employed in shrine construction, sacred tools, and pilgrimage tokens.

This moderate position on salvage appears to reflect economic necessity rather than pure theological principle—a tension acknowledged within internal God-Sleeper debates.

---

## Pilgrimage Implications

### Route Planning

God-Sleeper scouts maintain territory maps tracking warband movements, enabling route planning that minimizes encounters during aggressive periods. The Boar Moon cycle is avoided when possible, as warband behavior becomes notably more aggressive during this period.

Pilgrimage groups typically number two dozen or more—large enough to appear non-threatening while providing mutual protection. Advance offerings of iron scrap are deposited along planned routes a day before passage.

### Encounter Outcomes

During the observation period, documented pilgrimage encounters showed a high rate of peaceful resolution, with aggressive encounters representing a small minority. Pilgrim casualties did occur—deaths and injuries both—though warband casualties remained at zero due to defensive doctrine preventing counter-kills.

### Testimonials and Miracles

Some pilgrims report warbands "parting" to allow passage—stories interpreted as evidence that the Forge-Gods command their guardians to recognize the faithful. These accounts remain anecdotal without systematic documentation, but their cultural impact is significant: such stories reinforce doctrine and encourage continued pilgrimage despite acknowledged risks.

## Inter-Factional Relations

### vs. Iron-Bane Crusade

The fundamental conflict between God-Sleepers and Iron-Bane centers on their opposing characterizations of Svin-fylking. Iron-Bane Crusaders hunt what they call "Iron-Cursed beasts," while God-Sleepers protect what they call "Forge-Beast guardians."

Flashpoint regions occur primarily in the Jötunheim borderlands where both factions claim sacred geography. Violence remains rare but has increased in recent seasons, with confirmed clashes occurring in the past two years. The rhetorical divide is stark: Iron-Bane call God-Sleepers "beast-worshippers," while God-Sleepers call Iron-Bane "blasphemers."

### vs. Rust-Clan Hunters

A more pragmatic coexistence exists with Rust-Clan hunters. God-Sleepers accept hunting as a "necessary evil" conducted by those outside their faith, and notably purchase consecrated tusk and bone from Rust-Clan sources.

Conflict emerges primarily around shrine sites, where God-Sleepers object to hunting activity. Informal agreements have established no-hunt buffers around major shrines, though enforcement relies on mutual goodwill rather than formal authority.

### vs. Rangers Guild

The Rangers Guild maintains professional neutrality in theological disputes while acknowledging God-Sleeper territorial knowledge. Information exchange occurs—Rangers provide warband movement data to pilgrimage planners—but the relationship remains transactional rather than ideological.

## Internal Debates

### Aggression Problem

Boar Moon aggression presents a doctrinal challenge: if Svin-fylking are blessed guardians, why do they attack the faithful? Explanations range from "the Gods test us through their guardians' fury" to "human encroachment provokes righteous defense." The tension remains unresolved, as casualties undermine the "blessed protector" narrative.

### Salvage Ethics

Debate continues regarding salvage permissibility. Conservatives argue that no salvage is appropriate from sacred creatures, even naturally shed materials. The dominant moderate position permits found materials while prohibiting hunting—a compromise driven by economic necessity given the value of Iron Husk components.

---

## Strategic Implications

Doctrine-based protocols appear to reduce but not eliminate pilgrimage risk. Successful peaceful encounters validate belief and attract converts, while the Creed's geographic concentration in warband territories provides defensive advantage against Iron-Bane incursion.

The long-term sustainability of current doctrine remains uncertain. Increasing warband populations may eventually force revision of encounter protocols, particularly if casualty rates rise.
