# Sanctuary Establishment Record — Warden-Host Territorial Governance and Protocol

## Observation Context

**Compiled by:** Jötun-Reader Diplomatic Division, Factional Analysis Bureau
**Observation period:** Direct observation and document analysis, Year 700-783 PG; historical reconstruction to Year 340 PG
**Methodology:** Administrative document review, site inspection, officer interviews, territorial mapping, Iron-Bane conflict incident analysis
**Purpose:** Comprehensive record of Warden-Host sanctuary establishment procedures, governance structures, and territorial disputes — with emphasis on Void-Marked protection protocols and Iron-Bane opposition

---

## §1 — The Grith-Hold Charter (Year 340 PG)

The founding document of the Warden-Host — the Grith-Hold Charter — established the legal and philosophical framework for all subsequent sanctuary operations. Core provisions:

**Article I — Universal Sanctuary Right:** "Any person who presents themselves at the gates of a Sanctuary of the Host, seeking protection from violence, persecution, or expulsion on grounds of condition, origin, or belief, shall receive shelter, sustenance, and the protection of the Host's arms. No condition of entry shall be imposed beyond the oath of peaceful conduct within Sanctuary walls."

**Article II — Armed Neutrality:** "The Host shall maintain sufficient arms and trained personnel to defend its Sanctuaries against any force that threatens those under its protection. The Host shall not initiate offensive military action against any faction, Hold, or individual. Defence is the sole legitimate use of Host arms."

**Article III — Self-Governance:** "Each Sanctuary operates under Host law, independent of local Jarl authority, factional governance, or external legal jurisdiction. Sanctuary law is promulgated by the Council of Wardens and administered by Sanctuary Stewards. No external authority shall exercise legal power within Sanctuary boundaries."

**Historical significance:** The Charter predated the first large-scale Void-Marked population by approximately 60 years. The original protected populations were refugees from inter-Hold conflicts, CPS patients exiled from conservative Holds, and political dissidents fleeing factional persecution. The Charter's deliberately broad language — "condition, origin, or belief" — proved critical when the Void-Marked emerged as a distinct population requiring institutional protection.

## §2 — Sanctuary Establishment Protocol

The Warden-Host follows a standardised five-phase process for founding new sanctuaries:

### Phase 1 — Need Assessment (3-6 months)

Council of Wardens evaluates:

- Population pressure on existing sanctuaries (capacity threshold: 85% occupancy triggers assessment)
- Emergence of new refugee populations or persecution patterns
- Geographic coverage gaps in the sanctuary network
- Political feasibility — will the local Jarl or dominant faction tolerate Host presence?

### Phase 2 — Site Selection (6-12 months)

Site requirements:

- Defensible terrain with natural barriers (elevated ground, water features, narrow approaches)
- Agricultural potential sufficient for population of 300-800 residents
- Water source (minimum 2 independent sources for redundancy)
- Distance from active Blight zones (ambient Aetheric reading below 0.3 Kveld)
- Proximity to trade routes for supply chain maintenance
- Distance from Iron-Bane territorial boundaries (minimum 40 km buffer preferred)

### Phase 3 — Fortification (12-24 months)

Construction follows Host military engineering standards:

- Perimeter wall: stone and earthwork, minimum 4 metres height, with walkway and guard positions
- Gate complex: reinforced timber and iron, Spell-Lock secured, with kill-zone approach design
- Interior: residential quarters, medical facility, communal hall, food storage, artisan workshops
- Command post: Steward's quarters, signal room, armoury, archive

### Phase 4 — Population Establishment (6-12 months)

Initial population sourced from:

- Transfer residents from over-capacity sanctuaries
- Recruitment of skilled personnel (medical staff, artisans, farmers, teachers)
- Acceptance of first external sanctuary seekers
- Host garrison deployment (minimum 40 armed Wardens for a standard sanctuary)

### Phase 5 — Diplomatic Integration (ongoing)

- Establish formal communication with nearest Hold authority
- Negotiate trade agreements for goods the sanctuary cannot produce internally
- Register with Jötun-Reader mapping services for inclusion in route guides
- Coordinate with Rangers Guild for intelligence sharing and patrol integration

## §3 — Void-Marked Sanctuary Rights

Following the Iron Edict of Year 456 PG — the Iron-Bane Crusade's formal declaration classifying Void-Marked as purification targets — the Warden-Host expanded its protection framework specifically to address Void-Marked needs:

**Amendment to Article I (Year 458 PG):** Explicit enumeration of "Aetheric condition" as a protected category, removing any ambiguity about Void-Marked eligibility for sanctuary.

**Medical integration:** Sanctuary medical facilities maintain dedicated Void-Marked health monitoring programmes. Quarterly assessment for condition stability, long-term outcome tracking, and Weir-Bone diagnostic equipment maintenance.

**Labour integration:** Void-Marked residents participate in sanctuary economy as scouts, Aetheric hazard assessors, and perimeter monitors. Their unique sensory capabilities are treated as professional skills deserving fair compensation — not exploitable resources.

**Cultural accommodation:** Sanctuaries provide space for Void-Marked community gatherings, cultural practices, and support networks. The Host recognises that Void-Marked populations face psychological challenges (social stigma, identity adjustment, uncertain long-term prognosis) requiring community support infrastructure.

**Advocacy mission:** Host diplomats carry specific mandate to advocate for Void-Marked civil rights in negotiations with external Holds. Documented persecution incidents are compiled into annual reports distributed to Jötun-Reader archives and sympathetic Hold administrations.

## §4 — Echo-Cant Communication Protocols

The Warden-Host adapted acoustic communication principles for sanctuary operations:

**Warden Signal-Cant — Operational Specification:**

A coded acoustic communication system used for:

- Perimeter patrol coordination (guard rotation, threat detection signalling)
- Sanctuary-to-outpost messaging (short tactical transmissions over 200-500m range)
- Emergency alert cascades (standardised alarm patterns for different threat categories)

**Signal categories:**

| Signal Pattern                | Meaning                                 | Range         | Priority  |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------- |
| Three short clicks            | Patrol checkpoint — all clear           | 200m          | Routine   |
| Two long, one short           | Unidentified approach detected          | 300m          | Alert     |
| Sustained rapid clicks        | Armed force approaching                 | 500m          | Emergency |
| Alternating long-short        | Medical emergency — assistance required | 300m          | Urgent    |
| Five short, pause, five short | All-sanctuary assembly — Steward's call | Full compound | Command   |

**Acoustic perimeter arrays:** Three frontier sanctuaries (Grith-Hold, Fell-Watch, Stone-Vigil) employ passive ground-vibration monitoring systems. These arrays detect approaching groups through seismic signature analysis, providing 15-30 minutes early warning depending on terrain composition and group size.

**Training integration:** All Warden recruits complete Signal-Cant proficiency certification during initial training (8-week programme). Advanced operators — typically those with Ranger Guild background — serve as signal specialists responsible for array maintenance and protocol development.

## §5 — Iron-Bane Conflict Record

The Warden-Host's most persistent external challenge is the Iron-Bane Crusade's opposition to Void-Marked sanctuary:

**Major incidents:**

- **Year 456 PG — The Iron Edict:** Crusade formally classifies Void-Marked as purification targets. Direct catalyst for Warden-Host protection expansion
- **Year 478 PG — The Fell-Watch Siege:** Iron-Bane forces besieged a frontier sanctuary for 31 days before withdrawing due to supply exhaustion. First test of Host armed neutrality doctrine under combat conditions. Zero casualties on either side — the Crusade declined to assault fortified positions
- **Year 512 PG — The Border Accord:** Informal agreement between Shield-Warden Bjorn Grith-Born and Iron-Bane Commander Sigurd Anvil-Sworn establishing a 40 km buffer zone between Crusade territory and sanctuary perimeters. Reduced incidents by ~60% for two decades
- **Year 534 PG — Accord Breakdown:** New Crusade leadership repudiated the Border Accord. Resumed patrols and harassment of Void-Marked individuals in buffer zone territory
- **Year 773 PG — Current posture:** Low-intensity friction. Periodic border incidents (2-4 per year). Iron-Bane propaganda campaigns labelling the Host as "Blight sympathisers." No large-scale military action since Year 478 PG

**Host response doctrine:** Armed defence of sanctuary boundaries; armed escort for Void-Marked individuals in contested territory; diplomatic engagement through Jötun-Reader intermediaries; documentation and publication of all persecution incidents. No offensive operations — the Charter prohibits them.

**Active sanctuaries:** 17 (12 Midgard, 4 Jötunheim borderlands, 1 Svartalfheim approaches)
**Total personnel:** ~2,800 (1,200 armed Wardens, 400 medical staff, 1,200 administrative/support)

**Shield-Warden:** Astrid Grith-Born (third term, appointed Year 773 PG)
**Annual operating cost:** Equivalent to sustaining a medium-sized Hold — funded through sanctuary agricultural production, artisan trade, and diplomatic contributions from sympathetic Holds
