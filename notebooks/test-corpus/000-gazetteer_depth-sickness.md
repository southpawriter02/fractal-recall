# 🌀 Depth-Sickness: Psychological and Physiological Effects of Prolonged Subterranean Exposure

## §1: Phenomenon Overview & Classification

**Depth-Sickness** is a complex psychophysiological condition affecting humans during prolonged exposure to deep subterranean environments, typically manifesting at depths exceeding 1500 meters below surface reference points. The condition presents as progressive sensory disorientation, psychological distress, and physiological symptoms that escalate with increased depth and duration of exposure.

**Primary Characteristics:**
- **Onset depth threshold:** 1500-2000m for unacclimated personnel (varies by individual physiology)
- **Symptom progression:** Mild disorientation → moderate anxiety/nausea → severe hallucinations/panic
- **Recovery pattern:** Symptoms resolve during controlled ascent; typically complete recovery within 24-48 hours of surface return
- **Recurrence risk:** Individuals experiencing severe episodes show 60-70% likelihood of recurrence on subsequent deep traverses

**Diagnostic Significance:**
Depth-Sickness represents the human body's multifaceted response to extreme subterranean conditions: atmospheric pressure changes, sensory deprivation, circadian rhythm disruption, and psychological strain from enclosed environments. Unlike altitude sickness (rapid ascent trauma), Depth-Sickness emerges gradually during descent, making it insidious threat requiring proactive monitoring rather than reactive treatment.

**Strategic Impact:**
The phenomenon establishes operational depth ceilings for extended human presence in The Deep, affecting Dvergr mining operations, Jötun-Reader research expeditions, Rangers Guild transit security, and Silent Folk waystation deployment feasibility. Understanding onset thresholds and mitigation protocols is essential for safe deep-environment operations.

## §2: Physiological Mechanisms & Causative Factors

### Atmospheric Pressure Effects

**Pressure Gradient:**
Subterranean atmospheric pressure increases approximately 1 atmosphere per 100 meters of depth (standard model; varies with geological composition and ventilation systems). At 1500m depth, personnel experience ~15 atmospheres pressure (15x surface baseline).

**Physiological Responses:**
- **Nitrogen narcosis analog** — Increased partial pressure of nitrogen in bloodstream affects neurotransmitter function, creating mild euphoria (500-1000m) progressing to impaired judgment and disorientation (1500m+)
- **Oxygen toxicity risk** — At extreme depths (>2000m), elevated oxygen partial pressure can induce seizures, respiratory distress, visual disturbances
- **Barometric stress** — Inner ear pressure equalization difficulties cause vertigo, nausea, spatial disorientation
- **Tissue compression** — Prolonged high-pressure exposure affects joint function, circulation, cellular respiration efficiency

**Dvergr Physiological Advantage:**
Dvergr populations exhibit superior pressure tolerance due to generations of deep-environment adaptation. Guild miners routinely operate at 1800-2200m depths without Depth-Sickness symptoms, suggesting genetic or epigenetic pressure-response modifications absent in baseline human populations.

### Sensory Deprivation & Circadian Disruption

**Environmental Monotony:**
Deep subterranean environments exhibit extreme sensory uniformity: constant temperature (8-12°C regardless of season), near-total darkness (absent artificial illumination), minimal acoustic variation (geological white noise), unchanging air quality (stale, mineral-laden).

**Psychological Consequences:**
- **Time perception distortion** — Absence of day/night cycles disrupts circadian rhythm; personnel lose accurate time sense within 72-96 hours of continuous deep exposure
- **Sensory hunger** — Brain seeks novel stimuli; absence creates hypervigilance to minor environmental changes (water drips, stone temperature variations, air current shifts)
- **Pattern hallucinations** — Visual cortex generates phantom images in darkness (geometric patterns, faces in rock formations, movement in peripheral vision)
- **Auditory hallucinations** — Acoustic processing centers interpret ambient geological noise as voices, footsteps, whispers

## §3: Symptom Progression & Diagnostic Thresholds

### Stage 1: Mild Onset (1500-1700m, Days 1-5)

**Physical Symptoms:**
- Mild headaches (pressure-related, typically temple or forehead region)
- Fatigue disproportionate to physical exertion
- Slight nausea or appetite suppression
- Sleep disturbances (difficulty falling asleep, frequent waking)

**Psychological Symptoms:**
- Irritability or mood shifts
- Difficulty concentrating on complex tasks
- Mild time perception distortion (underestimating passage of hours)
- Increased startle response to sudden sounds

**Functional Impact:** Minimal. Personnel remain operational with full task performance capability. Symptoms often attributed to general expedition stress rather than recognized as Depth-Sickness onset.

**Intervention:** Monitor symptoms; maintain hydration and regular meal schedule; ensure adequate rest periods; avoid depth progression until symptoms stabilize.

### Stage 2: Moderate Progression (1700-2000m, Days 6-14)

**Physical Symptoms:**
- Persistent headaches requiring analgesic medication
- Vertigo or balance difficulties (inner ear pressure effects)
- Moderate nausea; possible vomiting
- Visual disturbances (mild blurriness, difficulty focusing, eye strain)
- Joint pain or muscle cramping

**Psychological Symptoms:**
- Anxiety escalation (persistent worry about depth, safety, group dynamics)
- Sensory hallucinations begin (phantom sounds, movement in peripheral vision)
- Time perception severely disrupted (losing track of days, confusing sleep/wake cycles)
- Emotional volatility (rapid mood swings, tearfulness, anger outbursts)
- Intrusive thoughts about entrapment or catastrophic scenarios

**Functional Impact:** Moderate. Task performance degraded 20-40%; judgment impairment risk (poor risk assessment, safety protocol lapses); personnel require supervision for complex operations.

**Intervention:** Mandatory evaluation by expedition medical officer or team lead; initiate controlled ascent if symptoms worsen; consider aborting deep traverse if mission-critical tasks unachievable.

### Stage 3: Severe Crisis (>2000m or prolonged exposure at 1700-2000m)

**Physical Symptoms:**
- Debilitating headaches unresponsive to medication
- Severe vertigo preventing independent movement
- Persistent vomiting leading to dehydration
- Visual hallucinations (walls appearing to breathe, surfaces rippling, geometric distortions)
- Respiratory distress (sensation of insufficient oxygen despite adequate air supply)
- Tremors or muscle spasms

**Psychological Symptoms:**
- Panic attacks (acute anxiety, hyperventilation, sense of impending death)
- Severe disorientation ("walls breathing," losing sense of up/down orientation)
- Auditory hallucinations indistinguishable from reality (voices giving commands, phantom conversations)
- Paranoid ideation (believing team members hostile, convinced of conspiracy)
- Dissociation (detachment from physical body, feeling of watching self from outside)

**Functional Impact:** Severe. Personnel non-operational; require constant supervision; may pose danger to self or team through impaired judgment or panic-driven actions.

**Intervention:** Immediate evacuation mandate. Controlled ascent at maximum safe rate (100m/hour pressure decompression limit); personnel may require sedation for safe transport; abort mission entirely; medical evaluation required before future deep deployment authorization.

## §4: Field Protocols & Management Strategies

**FIELD CARD: Depth-Sickness Prevention & Response Protocols**

### BEFORE — Pre-Expedition Screening & Preparation

- **Medical screening** — Exclude personnel with pressure sensitivity history (barotrauma, inner ear disorders, severe motion sickness), anxiety disorders (claustrophobia, panic disorder), or prior severe Depth-Sickness episodes
- **Acclimation protocol** — Graduated depth exposure for first-time deep personnel: 500m (2 days) → 1000m (3 days) → 1500m (5 days) before full deep deployment authorization
- **Psychological preparation** — Brief personnel on symptom recognition, normalize reporting (Depth-Sickness is physiological response, not personal weakness), establish buddy-monitoring system
- **Circadian maintenance plan** — Enforce artificial day/night cycle using time-keeping devices; mandate 8-hour sleep periods regardless of subjective time perception; schedule regular meal times
- **Emergency ascent routes** — Map egress corridors with depth markers every 100m; pre-position supply caches (water, analgesics, emergency rations) along ascent routes

### DURING — Active Symptom Monitoring & Escalation Response

- **Daily symptom surveys** — Every team member reports symptom inventory each evening: headache severity (0-10 scale), nausea (yes/no), sleep quality (1-5 scale), hallucinations (describe), anxiety level (0-10 scale)
- **Buddy monitoring system** — Paired personnel cross-check for behavioral changes indicating unreported symptoms (withdrawal, confusion, task performance degradation, emotional volatility)
- **Depth progression discipline** — No descent >100m/day when personnel showing Stage 1 symptoms; mandatory 24-hour stabilization period before continued descent
- **Pressure equalization breaks** — 15-minute rest stops every 2 hours during descent; personnel practice deliberate ear-clearing techniques (swallowing, yawning, jaw movements)
- **Sensory enrichment** — Counteract monotony through varied activities during rest periods (storytelling, music, tactical planning discussions, skill-sharing sessions)

**Escalation Thresholds:**
- **Stage 1 (Mild):** Monitor closely; maintain current depth; prohibit further descent until symptoms resolve
- **Stage 2 (Moderate):** Initiate controlled ascent (100m/hour); reassess every 200m elevation gain; abort mission if symptoms persist at 1200m elevation
- **Stage 3 (Severe):** Emergency evacuation; maximum safe ascent rate; entire team returns to surface with affected individual (no personnel left behind)

### AFTER — Post-Expedition Decompression & Recovery

- **Controlled ascent protocol** — 100m/hour ascent rate (prevents rapid decompression trauma); 30-minute rest stops every 500m elevation gain
- **Surface stabilization period** — Minimum 48-hour observation after surface return; personnel housed in well-lit, spacious quarters (counteract sensory deprivation effects)
- **Psychological debrief** — Expedition Medical Officer or trained counselor conducts individual interviews documenting symptom timeline, severity, recovery pattern
- **Medical clearance requirement** — Personnel experiencing Stage 2+ symptoms require formal medical evaluation before next deep deployment authorization
- **Recurrence risk assessment** — Individuals with severe episodes flagged for heightened monitoring; maximum depth ceilings imposed (e.g., personnel with Stage 3 episode at 1900m restricted to 1500m maximum on future deployments)

## §6: Factional Perspectives & Operational Adaptations

### Dvergr Clans — Genetic Adaptation & Pressure Tolerance

Dvergr Guild miners represent **biological control group** for Depth-Sickness research: a population with documented superior pressure tolerance enabling routine operations at depths causing severe symptoms in baseline humans.

**Proposed Mechanisms:**
- **Enhanced baroreceptor function** — Dvergr inner ear structures may exhibit superior pressure equalization capability
- **Neurotransmitter regulation** — Resistance to nitrogen narcosis effects suggests altered GABA or glutamate receptor sensitivity
- **Circadian rhythm flexibility** — Dvergr appear less affected by absence of day/night cycles, possibly due to generations living in low-light environments

**Cultural Perspective:**
Guild culture views Depth-Sickness in baseline humans as expected weakness rather than disorder, comparable to surface-dwellers' altitude sickness when ascending mountains. This creates occasional cultural friction when Dvergr overseers dismiss human symptoms as "soft-blooded surface complaint" rather than legitimate medical condition requiring intervention.

### Jötun-Readers — Medical Research & Acclimation Protocol Development

Jötun-Reader medical division treats Depth-Sickness as **critical research priority** due to implications for deep-environment exploration and Silent Folk study operations.

**Research Achievements:**
- Established graduated acclimation protocols reducing Stage 2+ incident rates from 45% (unacclimated) to 12% (acclimated) among research personnel
- Identified individual susceptibility markers (inner ear sensitivity, anxiety baseline, prior pressure exposure history) enabling better pre-expedition screening
- Developed symptom severity scales and escalation thresholds codified in Field Card protocols used across multiple factions

**Operational Impact:**
Reader expeditions employ strictest symptom monitoring protocols in Aethelgard, with Expedition Medical Officers empowered to override leadership judgment and mandate evacuation based solely on clinical assessment. This conservative approach reduces severe incident rates but increases mission abort frequency (18% of deep expeditions terminated early due to medical concerns vs. 8% for Dvergr Guild operations).
