# 1.0 Purpose and Scope

This specification defines the **Dead-Light Sanctuary Chain**—a specialized network of occlusion-protected waypoints enabling safe navigation through dead-light eddy zones in Alfheim and other high-Aetheric environments.

The Sanctuary Chain provides:

1. **Safe Refuge Points:** Shielded cells offering protection from dead-light exposure and CPS-safe rest zones
2. **Hop Navigation:** Sequenced sanctuary-to-sanctuary movement enabling traverse of otherwise-impassable eddy fields
3. **Emergency Extraction:** Pre-positioned rescue infrastructure for teams experiencing veil failure or CPS onset
4. **Research Platforms:** Controlled observation points enabling incremental study of dead-light phenomena

# 3.0 Sanctuary Cell Architecture

## 3.1 Cell Design Standards

**Core Concept:** A sanctuary cell is a **permanently-installed occlusion shelter** providing 360-degree dead-light shielding.

**Physical Structure:**
- **Footprint:** 3-5 meter diameter circular or hexagonal floor plan
- **Height:** 2.5 meter minimum interior clearance (standing headroom)
- **Walls:** Dual-layer construction: outer structural shell + inner occlusion membrane
- **Roof:** Domed or peaked design with full occlusion coverage
- **Entry:** Single doorway with occlusion curtain providing airlock-style entry

**Occlusion Requirements:**
- **Shielding effectiveness:** ≥98% dead-light occlusion at all angles
- **Testing protocol:** Annual recertification using passive Aetheric sensors
- **Failure indicators:** Visible cracks in membrane, anchor point corrosion, fabric delamination

**Interior Amenities (standard configuration):**
- **Seating:** Benches or floor cushions for 4-6 personnel
- **Lighting:** Low-bleed LED strips (indirect, non-glare) with manual control
- **Storage:** Sealed lockers for emergency supplies, spare occlusion veils, first aid equipment
- **Documentation:** Cell log book recording transit dates, personnel counts, equipment status, anomaly reports
- **Vigil space** (God-Sleeper cells): Meditation area with anti-pattern textiles

## 3.2 Cell Types and Variants

**Type A: Standard Waypoint Cell**
- Purpose: Navigation waypoint for hop transit
- Capacity: 4-6 personnel
- Duration rating: 6-hour maximum occupation
- Spacing: Positioned at hop intervals (40-120m depending on eddy density)

**Type B: Extended Rest Cell**
- Purpose: Multi-hour rest and recovery
- Capacity: 6-10 personnel
- Duration rating: 24-hour occupation with ventilation system
- Spacing: Every 3-5 standard cells along chain
- Additional features: Sleep platforms, food storage, medical supplies

**Type C: Research Observatory Cell**
- Purpose: Controlled eddy observation and data collection
- Capacity: 2-4 personnel + instrumentation
- Duration rating: 8-hour shifts with crew rotation
- Special features: Matte-baffled observation port, passive sensor mounts, data recording equipment

**Type D: Emergency Extraction Cell**
- Purpose: CPS intervention and casualty stabilization
- Capacity: 2 casualties + 2 medical personnel
- Duration rating: 72-hour sustained operation
- Special features: Silent Room protocols, medical supplies, emergency beacon, communication equipment

## 3.3 Cell Numbering and Identification

**Chain Designation System:** Each sanctuary chain receives unique identifier (e.g., "Alfheim-SC-01") followed by cell numbers.

**Marking Requirements:**
- **Exterior:** Matte-painted cell number on door (tactilely embossed for zero-light identification)
- **Interior:** Cell number, chain designation, next/previous cell directions, emergency procedures posted on wall
- **Direction Markers:** Each cell displays clear directional information with distance and bearing

# 4.0 Sanctuary Hop Protocols

## 4.1 Hop Navigation Concept

**Fundamental Principle:** Sanctuary hop navigation involves **sequential cell-to-cell movement** through eddy zones, with each "hop" representing a short, veil-protected traverse between sanctuary cells.

**Hop Distance Standards:**
- Low eddy density: 80-120m hop intervals
- Moderate density: 50-80m hop intervals
- High density: 40-60m hop intervals
- Extreme density: 20-40m hop intervals with enhanced shielding

## 4.2 Pre-Hop Preparation (Inside Sanctuary Cell)

1. **Rest and cognitive assessment** (30-60 minutes):
   - All personnel complete minimum 30-minute rest in low-stimulus environment
   - Medical Lead administers brief CPS screening

2. **Route briefing:**
   - Lead Reader reviews next hop: distance, bearing, known eddy locations, veil configuration
   - Identify abort gates and maximum hop duration

3. **Equipment inspection:**
   - Occlusion veils: Full inspection for tears and anchor integrity
   - Safety lines: Check for abrasion
   - Emergency hoods: Verify each person carries one with 3-second deployment capability
   - Communication: Test voice communication and establish verbal check-in cadence

4. **Role assignment:**
   - Lead Navigator: Maintains bearing, identifies next cell
   - Occlusion Technician: Monitors veil stability during hop
   - Watcher: Observes all personnel for fixation symptoms
   - Medical Rear: Maintains line discipline, monitors for stragglers

5. **Final checks:**
   - Team count verification
   - Equipment manifest
   - Cell log entry (departure time, personnel count, destination)
   - Airlock exit (maintain occlusion curtain)

## 4.3 Hop Execution (Between Cells)

**Movement Protocol:**
1. Exit cell via airlock: One person at a time through double-curtain system
2. Establish physical contact: Hand-on-shoulder or safety line connection
3. Deploy occlusion veils: Occlusion Technician positions temporary veils along route
4. Crawl discipline: All movement at crawl speed (hands-and-knees)
5. Gaze discipline: Maintain downward gaze ≤2 meters ahead
6. Navigate toward next cell: Lead Navigator maintains bearing
7. Verbal check-ins: Every 30 seconds, Lead calls each name
8. Continuous veil monitoring: Every 2 minutes for anchor slippage or tears

**Hop Duration Limits:**
- Standard hops (50-120m): 10-15 minute maximum
- Short hops (20-50m): 5-10 minute maximum
- Extended hops (emergency): 20 minute absolute maximum

**Black-Flags (Immediate Abort to Previous Cell):**
- Veil failure: Anchor break, tear >5cm, gap in coverage
- Fixation detected: Glazed stare, verbal non-response, halted movement
- CPS symptoms: Headache, nausea, semantic aphasia
- Navigation failure: Cannot locate next cell after 50% overtime
- Environmental surge: Visible intensification of eddy glare

## 4.4 Arrival and Post-Hop Recovery (Inside Next Cell)

1. **Entry via airlock:** One person at a time
2. **Immediate headcount:** Verify all personnel present
3. **Fixation screening:** Watcher observes each person for 60 seconds
4. **Equipment inspection:** Check veils for damage
5. **Cell log entry:** Record arrival time, personnel count, equipment status, anomalies
6. **Rest period:** Minimum 45-60 minutes before next hop
7. **Medical assessment:** Brief CPS screening

**Recovery Standards:**
- Minimum inter-hop interval: 45 minutes
- Daily hop limit: Maximum 6 hops per 24-hour period
- Extended rest requirement: After 3 consecutive hops, minimum 3-hour rest in Extended Rest Cell
- Mandatory retreat triggers: Failure of 2 consecutive CPS screenings; cumulative hop duration >90 minutes in 6-hour period

# 5.0 Chain Maintenance and Operations

## 5.1 Inspection and Recertification Schedule

**Quarterly Inspection** (God-Sleeper sanctuary crews):
- Visual inspection of cell structure: walls, roof, floor integrity
- Occlusion curtain check: tears, anchor points, fabric condition
- Interior amenity status
- Cell log review: identify patterns
- Supply restocking: emergency hoods, first aid, spare batteries

**Annual Recertification** (Jötun-Reader occlusion specialists):
- Full occlusion testing: deploy passive Aetheric sensors; verify ≥95% shielding
- Membrane integrity: inspect for delamination, punctures, erosion
- Anchor load testing: verify all curtain anchors hold ≥50kg load
- Structural assessment: check for settling, cracks, leaks
- Certification placard: post recertification date

## 5.2 Supply Management

**Standard Cell Supplies** (inspected quarterly, restocked as needed):
- Emergency occlusion hoods: 6 units per standard cell
- First aid kit: trauma supplies, CPS intervention medications
- Spare batteries: LED replacements, beacon batteries
- Emergency rations: Journey-bread (72-hour supply)
- Water: Sealed containers (48-hour supply)
- Communication: Emergency beacon (manual activation)
- Documentation: Blank cell log pages

# 6.0 Personnel Requirements and Training

## 6.1 Minimum Team Composition

**Standard Hop Team** (3-6 personnel):
- **Lead Reader:** Navigation authority, abort decisions
- **Occlusion Technician:** Veil management, shielding integrity
- **Medical Lead:** CPS screening, intervention authority
- **Watcher:** Fixation monitoring
- Optional: Additional technical specialists, research personnel

**Team Size Constraints:**
- Minimum: 3 personnel
- Optimal: 4-5 personnel
- Maximum: 6-8 personnel

## 6.2 Certification Requirements

**Sanctuary Hop Certification** (all personnel):
- Duration: 16 hours initial training + 8 hours annual refresher
- Topics: Dead-light characteristics, CPS escalation, occlusion veils, sanctuary hop protocols, fixation recognition, emergency abort, CPS self-monitoring

**Specialist Certifications:**
- **Lead Reader:** Navigation certification + 3 supervised hop transits
- **Occlusion Technician:** Advanced veil management + occlusion testing practicum
- **Medical Lead:** Silent Room protocols + CPS intervention training
- **Research Observer:** Observatory cell operations + instrumentation certification (Type C cells only)

# 7.0 Factional Perspectives and Operational Doctrines

**Jötun-Readers:** Treat chains as essential research infrastructure. Maintain detailed transit logs, compile CPS incidence data. Conservative protocols: strict rest intervals, maximum daily hop limits, immediate abort on black-flag.

**God-Sleepers:** View sanctuary cells as sacred spaces. Maintain majority of cells; consider maintenance a sacred duty. Expand chains to heal "Yggdrasil's wounds." Some groups use cells for controlled eddy exposure rituals.

**Rangers Guild:** Developed "sprint hop" protocols for time-critical missions. Reduce rest intervals to 30 minutes and daily hop limits to 8-10. Elite personnel only, with strict CPS monitoring.

**Dökkálfar (Echo-Born):** Can tolerate longer hop durations and shorter rest intervals. May show delayed-onset CPS 12-24 hours post-transit. Require 72-hour medical monitoring regardless of immediate symptoms.

**Scavenger Barons:** View chains pragmatically as economic infrastructure. Invest when salvage returns justify infrastructure costs. Often operate at minimum viable safety margins.
