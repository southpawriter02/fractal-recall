## Diagnostic Summary

**Weir-Bone** is a naturally occurring organic-mineral composite material found in deep geological strata, primarily within Nidavellir mining zones and the lower reaches of Jötunheim's industrial ruins. The material exhibits unique Aetheric resonance properties: it passively absorbs, channels, and dampens Aetheric energy with an efficiency unmatched by any synthesized alternative. Its name derives from the Old Norse _vér_ (sacred enclosure) and its structural resemblance to fossilised bone — though it is neither sacred in origin nor biological in composition.

Weir-Bone occupies a critical position in post-Glitch material economy. It is the primary natural substitute for pre-Glitch synthetic Aetheric crystals, used in degraded Spell-Lock construction, medical shielding equipment, Void-Marked diagnostic tools, and Dvergr resonance engineering. Its rarity and strategic importance make it one of the most contested salvage commodities across all realms.

## §1 — Physical Properties

**Structural composition:** Weir-Bone consists of interlocking calcium-silicate crystalline fibres arranged in concentric laminar layers, superficially resembling fossilised cortical bone. Micro-analysis reveals embedded Aetheric trace particles distributed uniformly throughout the crystal matrix — the source of its resonance properties.

**Aetheric resonance behaviour:**

- **Passive dampening:** Raw Weir-Bone reduces ambient Aetheric field strength by 40-60% within a 30cm radius. This property makes it invaluable for medical shielding (Harrow-Sick treatment garments, Blight-exposure protective equipment)
- **Resonance channeling:** When shaped into precise geometric forms by skilled artisans, Weir-Bone can direct and focus Aetheric energy along predictable pathways. This property underlies its use in Spell-Lock resonance cores
- **Signal storage:** Small Weir-Bone fragments can retain Aetheric resonance impressions for extended periods (weeks to months), functioning as crude recording media for Aetheric signatures

**Physical durability:** Harder than standard bone but more brittle than metal. Shatters along laminar planes under impact stress but resists gradual compression. Working temperature range: -40°C to 450°C (above which crystalline structure degrades irreversibly). Insoluble in water; slowly dissolved by strong acids.

**Visual identification:** Cream-to-amber coloration with visible laminar banding. Faint pearlescent lustre under directed light. Pale blue luminescence visible in total darkness — a consequence of residual Aetheric energy emission.

## §2 — Sourcing and Harvesting

**Primary deposits:** Nidavellir deep strata, typically 800-1,500 metres below surface in zones adjacent to geological fault lines. Formation mechanism unknown — Dvergr mineralogists hypothesise the material crystallises at interfaces between natural geological formations and pre-Glitch Aetheric infrastructure buried during the Glitch.

**Harvesting difficulty:** Extraction requires specialist technique. Conventional mining tools damage Aetheric trace particle distribution, degrading resonance properties. Dvergr Guild-certified extractors use resonance-guided hand tools that follow laminar boundaries, preserving crystalline integrity.

**Yield rates:** A typical deep-strata vein produces 2-5 kilograms of usable Weir-Bone per 100 metres of tunnel face. High-quality specimens (suitable for Spell-Lock cores) constitute approximately 15% of raw yield.

**Supply dynamics:** Dvergr Guild controls the majority of primary extraction. Secondary supply flows through Rust-Clan salvagers who recover fragments from collapsed pre-Glitch infrastructure, and Scavenger Baron teams operating in Jötunheim's deeper ruins.

## §3 — Applications

**Medical shielding:** Woven Weir-Bone filaments create personal protective garments that reduce Aetheric exposure by 40-60%. Primary application: Harrow-Sick Stage 1 treatment and prevention for salvage workers in Blight-adjacent zones. The Warden-Host maintains the largest non-Dvergr stockpile for medical use.

**Spell-Lock components:** Weir-Bone fragments serve as the primary post-Glitch substitute for synthetic Aetheric crystals in Spell-Lock resonance cores. Performance: approximately 40% of pre-Glitch crystal resolution, but sufficient for artisan-grade security applications.

**Void-Marked diagnostics:** Weir-Bone proximity testing is the standard field method for Void-Marked identification. Contact between Weir-Bone and Void-Marked skin produces visible intensification of Void-Line luminescence — a non-invasive, reliable diagnostic indicator.

**Dvergr resonance engineering:** Guild engineers incorporate Weir-Bone components into precision instruments: resonance measurement tools, Aetheric field mapping devices, and tuning forks for runic calibration.

**Cultural and decorative:** High-quality Weir-Bone specimens are carved into prestige objects — officer insignia, guild tokens, and ceremonial artifacts. The material's luminescent properties and symbolic association with Aetheric mastery make it culturally valued beyond utilitarian applications.

## §4 — Salvage Interactions and Material Conflicts

**Void-Marked interaction:** Direct skin contact between Weir-Bone and Void-Marked individuals produces a mutual resonance amplification. The Weir-Bone's luminescence intensifies; the Void-Marked individual reports temporary enhancement of Aetheric perception. This interaction is benign and has led to specialised Void-Marked salvage teams employing Weir-Bone focus pieces to enhance their detection capabilities during deep-ruin operations.

**Iron Husk incompatibility:** Weir-Bone and Iron Husk material generate conflicting resonance patterns when brought into close proximity (<10cm). The interference produces an audible high-frequency whine and progressive degradation of both materials' Aetheric properties. This incompatibility creates practical challenges in mixed-material salvage contexts — combined storage or transport damages both commodities.

**Salvage priority conflicts:** Weir-Bone's high value and limited supply create inter-factional competition. Dvergr Guild claims primary extraction rights through historical precedent; Scavenger Barons assert territorial claims on surface-accessible deposits; Rust-Clans treat deep-ruin fragments as legitimate freelance salvage. No centralised arbitration mechanism exists, leading to periodic conflicts.
